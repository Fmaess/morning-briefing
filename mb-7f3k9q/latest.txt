MORNING BRIEFING – Friday, 27 November 2026

Happy Friday, legend. Let's close this week out strong.

Today's thesis: a violent relief bounce is running through Asia and the futures, but it's sitting on top of a very nervous tape, so don't mistake a bounce for a regime change.

Here's the setup. Asia is ripping. The Kospi jumped three point eight percent, Shanghai is up two point two, the Nikkei two, the Hang Seng one point nine. The S&P future is up one point eight percent and the Nasdaq future almost two point eight. BUT remember what this bounces off. The Nasdaq 100 is down nearly four percent over five days, and the dollar is lingering near a twenty-two-month peak as AI worries simmer. The headlines speak of Nvidia turmoil, a credit rating on watch, and a shares drop two days after Jensen Huang's return. So this is a market buying a dip in its biggest theme, not one that has resolved the doubt.

And the Kospi is the tell. Korea is the memory-chip economy, so a near four percent jump says the AI hardware trade is being bought back hard. But there's a wrinkle: Nvidia and Kioxia are reportedly teaming up on AI server memory as an alternative to South Korean chips. So it's a bounce with a question mark over the supply chain. Add that the Bank of Korea just hiked to three point two five percent, and you see a strong economy and tighter money at the same time.



Now the complication, and this is where it gets interesting. Copper is up almost seven percent to five point seven dollars, and gold is up over four percent to about thirty-six eighty. Those two normally don't rally together. Copper says growth and AI build-out. Gold says people want protection. And the China story fits: factories are growing profits again, but the AI boom is picking the winners, while consumption and real estate lag. So Chinese demand is two-speed, which favors industrial and capex names over consumer exposure.

Then energy. Brent is at ninety-two point eight dollars, down almost two percent today, but still elevated. There's a drone attack in Kuwait, and Trump says the Iran war ends "whenever I want it." Gas in Europe, TTF, is at eighty-seven point six euros, up almost four percent on the week. So energy stays a margin tax on European industry, BUT the geopolitical premium keeps it sticky, SO any rally in cyclicals has to fight that headwind.

Rates matter too. US ten-year yields fell about six basis points to four point eight percent, which helps the bounce. But Bund yields rose: the ten-year at three point three, the two-year at three point one. Europe is repricing the other way, so funding costs for small and mid caps aren't easing. Add a new ECB chief: Spain's De Cos was picked, and markets read that as continuity. That's calming, but it's not a catalyst.

And Europe already ran yesterday. The DAX gained two point one percent, the Stoxx 600 one point eight, on easing tariff fears. BUT the US is planning fresh tariff threats on fourteen trading partners, and Asia is rejecting the basis. So some of that relief may be fragile.

Also, US GDP got a huge upward revision, and Trump wants it investigated. Strong growth plus higher yields plus a strong dollar is not an easy mix for risk. It's a reason the VIX, though down five percent, still sits at twenty-six point eight. That's not complacency.

So land it in Europe. Historically, on mornings like this, the model points to the Stoxx 600 opening about half a percent higher, driven mostly by the Nikkei and Hang Seng. It's a rule of thumb, not a forecast, and it explains only about a fifth of the variance. Within that, technology has tended to outperform the market, industrials slightly, and commodity names strongly, tied to Hang Seng strength. Luxury has tended to get a lift from Hong Kong too, though modestly. On the other side, healthcare has tended to lag as the Kospi surges and money leaves defensives. Telecom, utilities and real estate have tended to underperform, which is classic rotation out of bond proxies when risk comes back on.

The crowding question: is that rotation structural or tactical? With the Nasdaq down on the week and yields in Europe rising, I'd call it tactical positioning. Defensives were the shelter, and shelters get sold in a bounce. If US cash equities fail to follow the futures, those underweights in healthcare and utilities look like the cheap hedge to rebuild.

What would change the picture? A fresh Nvidia headline, an oil spike from the Gulf, or tariff language over the weekend. Also, it's Black Friday in the US, so liquidity will be thin, which can exaggerate moves.



The take-away: it's a bounce, not a breakout, so respect the rally but keep the hedges.

That's a wrap for the week. Square your book, then open a cold one.
