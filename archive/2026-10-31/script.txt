Morning, big dog. Coffee in hand? Good. Let's do this.

It's Saturday, October 31st, and the dominant theme is simple: the AI trade is hitting a funding wall, just as oil climbs back toward highs and keeps rates from helping.

Start with Friday's tape. The Nasdaq 100 fell three and a half percent. The S&P 500 lost one point eight. The VIX jumped almost thirteen percent to just above nineteen. That's a jolt, not a panic. The Nasdaq is now on its first three-week losing streak since March. The reason in the headlines: hyperscaler projections this week raised questions about free cash flow and borrowing costs. Add S&P Global moving Meta to a negative outlook on capex and cash flow concerns. So the chain runs like this. Huge AI spending leads to thinner free cash flow. Thinner free cash flow, plus a market already wary of funding costs, means investors start to price the AI build-out like a leveraged project rather than a growth story. That hits the whole complex.

Asia saw it first. The Kospi fell three and a half percent, the Nikkei two point one. Both are heavy in chips and AI hardware. But note the Kospi is still up three percent over five days, so this looks like profit-taking in a crowded winner. There's a counterpoint in the Nikkei Asia headlines: memory makers are out-earning big tech on AI demand, and Kioxia is guiding to a huge profit jump. So demand isn't the worry. The worry is whether the customers can keep paying for it. That's the open question.

China is a different story. The Shanghai Composite fell one point four percent, and factory activity contracted in October, the third contraction in four months. The Hang Seng rose one point five percent anyway. That suggests investors are leaning on policy hopes, while the data says recovery is faltering. One headline noted Chinese shipping and coal stocks outperforming AI as earnings outlooks diverge. Rotation is happening there too.

Now the macro layer. Brent rose two point four percent to a hundred seventeen dollars sixty, six-month highs. The headline driver is attacks around Hormuz threatening the Iran peace effort. There's a counter-headline too: Qatari mediators held more than twelve hours of talks in Tehran to avoid a resumption of American-led strikes. So the oil price carries a binary risk. A deal could knock a chunk of risk premium out quickly. A breakdown pushes it higher. European gas is even more telling: TTF rose five point four percent, up more than ten percent on the week, to about a hundred and four. That matters for Europe's industrial margins more than Brent does.

Here's the nuance on rates. US ten-year yields fell six basis points to four point nine percent, and the five-year fell eight. Normally that's a relief for tech. This time it's a flight-to-safety move, not a good-news move, and yields at this level still mean heavy funding costs. Bund yields barely moved: ten-year at three point four percent, two-year at three point one. Gold fell three point three percent, which is interesting. A risk-off day with falling gold suggests forced selling and dollar strength, or positioning being cut. Copper fell one point nine percent, consistent with the China data. Euro-dollar at one point one two.

Earnings were light. ExxonMobil reported earnings per share of four twenty-three against four oh four expected, a beat of almost five percent, and the stock rose about two percent. Beyond the company, it fits the story. One headline notes oil majors' refining earnings surged as fuel supplies tightened. So energy is a cash-flow winner while tech's cash flow is under question. That's the rotation in a nutshell.

Now Europe. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell one point two percent, the DAX nearly one, the Stoxx 600 just over a third of a percent. Europe held up better, and it's up nearly two percent over five days. The sensitivity model, a historical rule of thumb and not a forecast, points to the Stoxx 600 opening roughly twenty basis points lower after a US drop like this. It also suggests oil and gas and the broader resources group have tended to outperform the market on days like this. Energy gets a tailwind from crude and the Exxon read-across.

Headwinds: European tech and semicap names tied to the AI build-out, and industrials and chemicals exposed to gas at a hundred and four. Banks are neutral to slightly positive on elevated Bund yields, though risk-off tone caps that. Defensives are the natural landing spot, though that's positioning, not yet a structural shift. A caution on France: headlines say it braces for fresh unrest, with the government promising a tough response. That's a risk for French assets.

Watch next week: Apple and AMD earnings, the jobs report, and the US midterms. Also whether Iran talks hold. What changes the picture is an oil deal, or a calmer tech tape.

The takeaway: the AI trade isn't broken on demand, it's being repriced on funding, while oil quietly redistributes who earns the cash.

That's your edge for today. Use it wisely, champ.
